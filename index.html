Shuxun Tian (田树旬)

Lecturer · Supervisor of Master's Students

Department of Astronomy, School of Physics and Astronomy, Beijing Normal University

I earned a doctoral degree in theoretical physics from Wuhan University in 2020 and then conducted postdoctoral research at Beijing Normal University. In 2024, I joined the Department of Astronomy at Beijing Normal University as a lecturer. My research focuses on developing fundamental formulations of gravity and applying them to cosmology and astrophysics. I proposed gravitational caloric theory. In recent years, I have also investigated observational tests of gravity in extremely weak-field regimes.

Gravitational Caloric Theory

S. X. Tian, Gravitational caloric theory: From early dark energy to a wide variety of gravitational phenomena, Phys. Rev. D 114, 044042 (2026). arXiv:2608.07318.

Gravitational caloric theory extends general relativity by introducing a vector field sourced by and coupled to the fluid sector. The initial motivation for the theory was to use the cosmic radiation-matter transition to trigger early dark energy (a candidate but very controversial new-physics approach to the Hubble tension), thereby solving the associated coincidence problem. The theory also exhibits a broad range of cosmological and astrophysical applications, and may even harbor deeper structures in both theoretical physics and mathematics (an intuition that has yet to be explored).

Ongoing

Observational Tests of Gravitational Theories

Alternatives to general relativity may modify the properties of local gravitational systems. We explore observational probes of such new physics using individual sources, relying on existing or near-future facilities and targeting regimes of gravity and regions of parameter space that remain only weakly tested. We have not yet identified a sufficiently compelling probe. Current efforts focus on the extremely weak-field regime, where, in my view, observations might uncover new physics rather than merely tighten existing upper limits.
